    Huygens noticed it in 1665

    Metronome Sync

    Stand a crowd of metronomes on a board that can roll, set each to its own tempo, and they slowly fall into step. You choose how many there are and how far apart their tempos start. Then you watch.

    With sound on, every metronome ticks at its own pitch. You can hear the notes bunch up into a chord as they line up.

    How this works

    What you are watching

    Each metronome gives the board a tiny push every time it swings, and the board pushes every other metronome back. That small shared motion is enough to pull their beats together. This app models it with the Kuramoto equations, the standard math for oscillators that share one coupling signal. The metronomes stay out of step until the coupling is strong enough, then they join the beat in order of how close their tempo is to the crowd's.

    Reading the numbers

    • In sync is how aligned the swings are, from 0% (each on its own) to 100% (identical). It is the Kuramoto order parameter r, corrected for the alignment you would see by chance with this many metronomes.
    • Locked means a metronome keeps the same tempo as the biggest cluster and swings in step with it.
    • Group tempo is that cluster's shared tempo, with its traditional Italian name.
    • Not synced yet lists the metronomes still keeping their own pace. The number is the tempo each is actually running right now, which can differ from the one you set. The small figure beside it is how far that is from the group.
    • The label above each metronome shows its live tempo. The weight on its rod marks the tempo you set: higher means slower.

    Coupling

    The strength slider is measured against a threshold worked out from your actual tempos. At 1.00× the coupling is just strong enough for all of them to lock. Below that, the fastest and slowest never join. Auto-ramp raises coupling slowly through that zone so you see metronomes join one at a time.

    Try this

    • After the ramp finishes, widen the tempo range. Coupling stays where it was, so the extremes fall out of step and the all-lock mark moves.
    • Once everything is locked, lower the coupling to about 1.00× and drag one weight far from the others. It breaks away while the rest keep going. At high coupling the group drags it along.
    • Set the speed to 4× to see a long ramp in less time.
